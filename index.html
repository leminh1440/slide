AI in the Future

An argument about AI, built around three forces. The technology, the money and the power are moving together, and together they can stretch a bad year into a lost decade.

Hennessy (Minh) LeNovember 2026About 11 minutes
Hi, I'm Hennessy Le. This talk is about AI, but my starting point is simple: AI does not arrive into an empty room. It arrives into an economy that already has its own money problems and its own power struggles. Put those together and a bad year can stretch into a lost decade.

Framing

Three forces, one room

Each of these forces on its own is familiar, and each on its own is survivable. The argument is that they are arriving at the same time, and that each one makes the others worse. Most of us in this room are about to enter the job market this argument describes.

01

The technology

The useful question is what is left that people get paid for. Each earlier wave moved people up a floor. The open question is whether there is another floor to move to.

02

The money

The gains from a technology go to whoever owns the companies that deploy it. The people who use it at work don't share in them by default. That is a fact about ownership, and it is already settled.

03

The power

AI has weight. It runs on chips from a few factories, shipped through a few narrow places, powered by a lot of electricity. Whoever controls those controls the pace.

01 · The technology

Each wave took a different part of us

Automation does not take everything at once. It takes one layer of what people sell for a wage, and then the next.

Step one

Muscle

The tractor replaced the animal and the person walking behind it, and engines took the rest of the heavy work. The people displaced didn't vanish. They moved into work that used their heads, and that move took a generation.

Step two

Simple cognition

Calculators, then spreadsheets, then databases. Arithmetic, record-keeping and looking things up stopped being jobs and became features. People moved up again, into work that needed judgment, and we called it skilled because a machine couldn't do it.

Step three

Judgment

This is the floor AI is on now: writing, analysis, synthesis, reasoning through a problem. It is also the floor most of us in this room are training for. Every earlier wave had a floor above it to move to. Nobody has shown there's another one.

The argument so far

The gains follow ownership. That was settled before AI arrived.

So that's where the gains go. Next, how borrowing makes prices move, on the way up and on the way down.

How it inflates

A share is worth a hundred dollars

So the bank lends you fifty against it. You still hold the share, and now you hold cash too. When prices rise, your collateral rises, so you borrow more, and that borrowing pushes prices up again. On the way up, nothing about this feels risky.

The trigger

Then everyone needs cash at once

Rates rise, or taxes rise, or something makes a lot of people reach for money on the same day. To get cash you have to sell. One seller is nothing. A thousand sellers is a price.

The cascade

The debt does not fall with the price

The share drops to twenty-five. The fifty you owe stays fifty, still charging interest. So you sell too, which pushes the price down and forces the next person to sell. The asset falls and the debt doesn't, and that one-sidedness is what turns a fall into a cascade. Whoever arrived last, with borrowed money, is hurt worst.

This is the mechanism under a bubble. Say a share is worth a hundred dollars and the bank lends you fifty against it. That's the whole trick: on the way down, the asset falls and the debt doesn't. That one-sidedness is why it cascades. Whoever came in last, with borrowed money, gets hurt worst.

Interlude

Assets are not money.

On paper you can be rich. You still cannot pay for lunch with a share certificate — and the moment you most need to sell is usually the moment nobody wants to buy.

Let this one land. On paper you can be rich, and still not be able to pay for lunch. With that in mind: are today's prices too high?

Line one

Knowledge only goes up

Technology and know-how accumulate. We have bad decades, but we don't wake up having forgotten how to make electricity or build a factory. This line rises more or less forever.

Line two

Short cycles, about six years

Growth, downturn, recovery, growth again. Uncomfortable, survivable, familiar. This is the only cycle most of us have lived through as adults. Nobody seriously disputes this line or the one before it.

05 · The power

The chokepoint

Models run on chips. Chips come from a handful of places, and those places sit behind shipping lanes someone else can close. Power here means containers, factories and supply chains as much as carriers and missiles.

06 · Close

Prepare instead of predicting

Nobody knows which of these breaks first. The useful move is to be ready for several of them.

01

You are your own biggest asset

If you don't have savings yet, gold versus stocks can wait. The choice that matters now is where your time and skills are paid the most. The same skill is paid very differently in two places, and changing the place is usually faster than changing the skill.

02

Adaptability beats any single skill

Each wave takes another layer. Being able to learn the next thing matters more than how well you hold on to the current one.

03

Don't bet on one future
